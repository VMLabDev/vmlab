# C912. The audio row failed for two reasons, and neither was the host's load

Card #912 of the vmlab board. Draft D3 and open point O3 of
[acceptance-2026-11.md](../../../../docs/agents/acceptance-2026-11.md#d3-win3d-the-audio-row-fails-with-cpus-to-spare-follow-up-of-c447):
row S1 `audio` of the [adapter suite](../acceptance.md#adapter-suite) failed
on both templates with CPUs to spare, and was to be repeated on a host with
no other heavy job. It is the follow-up of [C447](C447.md).

On the quiet host the row still failed. Two causes were found and both are
fixed:

| Code | Cause | Where | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | `pw-record` lost whole quanta of the capture when its write to the capture file waited for the disk. The VM's sound had no break. | The check | The capture file is on tmpfs |
| F2 | A virtio-gpu context create kept a vCPU out of the guest for 5 to 17 ms. When that vCPU takes the sound card's interrupt, Windows refills a period of the cyclic buffer after the HDA model has read it again. | vmlab-hv | The context create runs on a thread of its own, without the device lock |

F1 is cause K4 of C447 ("frames lost in the PipeWire of the account vmlab or
in `pw-record`"), now located. F2 is one source of cause K1 of C447 (a late
guest period) that needs no host load at all.

sine.

## F1. The capture lost frames, not the VM

The first failure on the quiet host was one shift of three host quanta: 768
frames missing at 27.7 s of a windows-10 tone, with no underrun and no late
callback in vmlab-hv and no late wake of the guest's player.

Session `x1` captured each of 18 tones with four `pw-record` at once, all
linked to the same node `vmlab.<lab>.win`:

| Recorder | Capture file | Tones with a break |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| the check's | the checkout, btrfs | 6 of 18 |
| a second | the checkout, btrfs | 6 of 18, the same six, at the same place |
| a third | `/dev/shm`, tmpfs | 0 of 18 |
| a fourth | `/dev/shm`, tmpfs | 0 of 18 |

The six breaks were 28.1 to 29.4 s into the capture. A tone that is whole in
two captures was played whole, so vmlab-hv and the sound server delivered
every frame of all 18 tones. `pw-record` writes the file in the callback
that takes the sound server's buffers. The break comes about 30 s after the
first write, which is the commit interval of btrfs: the write waits, and the
recorder misses the quanta of that time. Not checked: which call in
`pw-record` waits.

Change: `tests/e2e/adapter/checks/130_audio.sh` captures into a directory of
its own under `/dev/shm` (`ADAPTER_AUDIO_TMP` names another memory-backed
directory) and moves the file to the stage directory after the count. Where
that directory cannot be made the capture goes to the stage directory as
before, with a note. `tone.host.json` has the file system of the capture
file (`capture_fs`), and the row names it.

This also explains the "two to three breaks a minute with CPUs to spare" of
K4 in C447 and the quantum shifts of R5 to R9: they were made by the check.

## F2. A context create kept the sound card's vCPU away

With the capture on tmpfs windows-10 passed 6 of 6, and windows-11 failed 6
of 50. Each failure was one or two shifts of whole guest periods, with 0
underruns, 0 late callbacks and 0 late wakes of the player.

vmlab-hv builds with timing logs (`bin/diag1.patch` to `diag4.patch`) caught
four of the failed tones, and three of them were read line by line. The
chain in each:

1. The guest sends `VIRTIO_GPU_CMD_CTX_CREATE` twice in a row, a virgl
   context and then a Venus context. It does so for each process that opens
   the adapter: 108 creates in the two boots and 14 tones of the sessions
   `d3` and `d4`, most of them in the first minutes after a boot.
2. vmlab-hv ran each create on the vCPU thread that kicked the control
   queue, under the device lock: median 7.1 ms, shortest 4.6 ms, longest
   17.3 ms.
3. The renderer makes the context in 0.2 to 1.1 ms. The rest is the look
   for the render worker of the new context, which a reset later waits for
   (`hv/crates/vmlab-hv-devices/src/virtio/gpu/teardown.rs`, commit
   `77668c83`). It reads `/proc/<pid>/stat` of every process of the host.
4. The vCPU is in an MMIO exit all that time and runs nothing of the guest.
   On windows-11 vCPU 3 takes the sound card's interrupt. In the failed
   tones it was in two creates in a row, or in a register read that did not
   return while another vCPU's create ran, and acknowledged the period
   interrupt 8.7 to 11.8 ms late. The thread did not wait for a host CPU: its run-queue wait
   in that window was 0 to 12 us.
5. Windows refills a period after its interrupt and has 10 ms for it. The
   HDA model had read the period again by then: one whole stale period (480
   frames) in two of the three tones, and a period that the guest then left
   out in the third.

Change (`hv/crates/vmlab-hv-devices/src/virtio/gpu/mod.rs`): the kick of a
context create returns at once. The context is made and its worker looked
for on a `gpu-deferred` thread without the device lock. The control queue
keeps its order: no chain behind the command is taken until it is answered,
and the thread that answers it serves the chains that waited. A device reset
waits for the thread's work and drops its answer with the queue.

Measured in session `f3`, one boot and 14 tones on windows-11, against the
three boots and 30 tones of the sessions `d2` to `d4` before the fix:

| | Before | With the fix |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tones | 30 | 14 |
| Failed tones | 4 | 0 |
| Kicks of the control queue that took more than 2 ms | 217, median 6.8 ms, longest 17.3 ms | 4, longest 5.8 ms |
| Period interrupts acknowledged more than 2 ms late | 13, longest 11.8 ms | 0 |
| Whole stale periods read by the HDA model | 2 | 0 |

The four kicks that are left are blob map and unmap commands (`0x208`,
`0x209`), which change a KVM memory slot and take every vCPU out of the
guest for 2 to 6 ms. They broke no tone.

windows-10 creates contexts less often and did not fail for F2 in 30 tones.

Regression of the change, with the vmlab-hv bundle of `e0d64bca`: a fresh
`windows-11` lab (`tests/e2e/run.sh --template windows-11 --host-gpu AMD1`)
passed 12 of 12 Done checks, and `tests/e2e/adapter.sh` with the checks
`pointer pointer-shapes pace tear-flip tear-blt duplication shared-texture
d2d d2d-tir draws latency audio` had 15 rows pass and none fail
(`ev/r1/windows-11/run-1/adapter/adapter.txt`). The browser rows and the
Wine tests were not run.

## What is left

| Code | Point |
| --- | --- |
| A1 | K1 of C447 under real overload (48 busy processes on 32 CPUs) is not changed by this card: a vCPU that loses its host CPU for longer than a period still makes a stale period. A1 of C447 (the HDA model holds its DMA for a period the guest has not refilled) is still the work for that, and needs a deeper host queue to be worth it. |
| A2 | The look for the render worker still reads every `stat` file of `/proc`. It no longer delays a vCPU, but a context create takes 5 to 17 ms longer than it must. `/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/children` would name the workers directly where the kernel has it. |
| A3 | Blob map and unmap stop every vCPU for 2 to 6 ms. Not a break at this load. |
| A4 | Not measured: a host sink with a clock of its own. vmlab-hv paces the HDA model by the host's monotonic clock and the null sink of this account runs on the same clock, so a drift between the two cannot show here. |
