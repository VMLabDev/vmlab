# C373. Hardware pointer rotation

The [pointer probe](../pointer.md) ran on helios with vmlab-hv, Venus
on AMD1 (`renderD130`), and the Windows 11 `26100.1742.11#win3d`
template. The signed Win3D 0.1.3 package reused the C356 user-mode
components. The running miniport's SHA-256 matched the packaged driver
version `100.6.101.58116`, and `HWCursor` was enabled.

The probe rotated Windows, held the arrow, I-beam and resize cursor at
four distinct positions, hid the cursor at a fifth and restored the
display mode. It checked the native cursor's position and hotspot, the RFB
shapes, the I-beam outline, the cursor-free screenshots and the cursor
in the recording. The physical scanout was 1024 × 768. The first arrow
was at desktop coordinates (200, 300) in each run.

| Display rotation | First arrow on the scanout | Arrow hotspot in the cursor image | Checks passed |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 0° | (200, 300) | (0, 0) | 7 of 7 |
| 90° | (300, 567) | (0, 63) | 7 of 7 |
| 180° | (823, 467) | (63, 63) | 7 of 7 |
| 270° | (723, 200) | (63, 0) | 7 of 7 |

The fresh clone returned to Windows setup recovery after its update
reboot. The probe used a private copy of the prepared C356 AMD1 guest
and waited for its desktop reboot before copying the package. The final
probe used the branch-built recorder, including the zero-hotspot fix
in `627b2b5f`. An earlier 270° recording showed the first arrow at the
correct location with a Windows notification over the test background.
The notification caused the flat-background check to reject those frames.

The guest trace, native and RFB events, screenshot, video and verdict for
each angle are under
`/home/vmlab/src/stc/card-373-work/amd-prepared/desktop-rotation-<angle>/pointer/`.
